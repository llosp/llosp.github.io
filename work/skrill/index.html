skrill

A weekly game that gets a group of friends to actually do the creative work nobody is asking them for.

overview.md

Nobody asks you to practice, so you don't.

Skrill is a weekly game for people who want to make things they are not paid to make. Each season you declare what you will do and how hard it is. You deliver proof. Everything stays blurred until a short call where the group reveals it together and hands each other points.

I designed the rules, the points economy, the interface and the mascot, and built the whole stack myself. It has run since June 2026 with the five of us it was built for.

why.md

I make games with an artist friend, and the work that keeps us sharp has no deadline: drawing for fun, a new track, a tool nobody asked for. It loses every time to the work that does have a client.

We already met every week to show what we had made, but a goal that only lives in your head is easy to drop, especially with no date on it. I needed something with a short loop, other people in it, and a reason to show up on a specific day.

Notion boards and ClickUp lists feel like work, the opposite of what I wanted. So the brief became a system with a deadline, a bit of rivalry and a reason to show up, built to feel like play, not a spreadsheet.

how-it-works/

what i decided, and why

  1. /01 everyone sees you delivered, nobody sees what
  2. /02 you set the difficulty before you know how the week goes
  3. /03 part of your score is given to you by the group
  4. /04 it looks silly on purpose

Four decisions carry most of the design.

01-declare-in-public-deliver-in-secret.md

everyone sees you delivered, nobody sees what

The reveal is the reason the meeting exists.

You declare a goal with what and by when, which is the cheapest nudge there is. When you deliver, the proof appears in everyone's feed, but blurred until the weekly call. You can see that your friends are working. You cannot see what they made.

That gives the group pressure without spoilers, and it turns a status check into a ritual. The call, called Skrill Time, is 15 minutes at most: everyone marks ready, the blur drops, you react and the points are handed out.

02-difficulty-is-the-price.md

you set the difficulty before you know how the week goes

Simple goals pay 2 points, complex ones pay 5.

Choosing the difficulty is part of declaring the goal, on the same screen. That is on purpose. If you could pick afterwards, everyone would call everything hard. Setting it up front makes you price your own ambition, and makes a failed complex goal cost something.

An extra tier pays no base points at all and scores only through the group, for things you do just because. Some seasons a bounty appears, a prompt for something to make, such as a sci-fi piece, a sticker for the group or a classic painting in your own style. The chance of one rises every season it does not show up, so they stay a surprise. If a friend takes it, you want to as well, because you do not want them to take all the points.

03-points-from-your-friends.md

part of your score is given to you by the group

Every person gets a small pool of bonus points to hand out.

The base points are fixed, so the interesting part is the bonus. Each person receives a pool equal to half the goals delivered that season, rounded down, and must spend all of it on the deliveries, anonymously. You can score yourself, but you are spending from the same pool.

The pool follows the deliveries, not the headcount, so a busy season has more bonus to give away than a thin one. This stops the leaderboard from being just a count of tasks. It rewards the work the group actually thought was good, and it keeps the reveal alive, because nobody knows who got what until the end.

04-deliberately-unserious.md

it looks silly on purpose

The friendliness is the product.

The pixel fonts, the Windows 95 windows and the mascot are not decoration. They are what keeps this from feeling like work. The mascot walks along the bottom of every page and can be thrown around with a click.

You cannot upload a profile photo. You have to draw one, in a small black and white pixel canvas. It is a limited, slightly clumsy tool, and that is the point.

what-changed.md

what i decided, and why

  1. /05 a goal you tried and missed can still score
  2. /06 a delivery only counts if nobody calls it fake
  3. /07 a bounty is worth more the fewer people take it

The four decisions above carry most of the design. Three more rules were added to it in the first ten days of June 2026.

05-trying-counts-for-something.md

a goal you tried and missed can still score

One point, and only if the group says so.

A goal can end as attempted, not just delivered or failed. During Skrill Time the group votes up or down on each attempt, and an attempt with more up votes than down gets 1 point. It is small next to the 2 or 5 a delivery pays, but it separates the week you tried and missed from the week you did nothing.

06-a-delivery-can-be-cancelled.md

a delivery only counts if nobody calls it fake

It takes everyone else, not just one person.

Any player can report someone else's delivery while the bonuses are being handed out. If every other participant reports it, the delivery is cancelled and the goal is marked as failed. A single report changes nothing, so one person cannot take points off another.

07-bounties-pay-less-when-shared.md

a bounty is worth more the fewer people take it

7 points alone, 4 with two, 3 with three, 2 after that.

A bounty pays by how many players deliver it. Going in alone is rewarded, and a bounty everyone takes is worth a little each instead of a lot. It is the rule behind not wanting a friend to take all the points.

outcome.md

  • 13 seasons revealed since june 2026, with the same five people
  • 119 goals declared across them
  • 85% delivered, 101 of 119

The system has held up for the people it was built for. The weekly call used to be a chat with nothing to count. Now it is a reveal of what each person made, with points on it. I make more than I did before it existed.
